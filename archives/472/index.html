A Few Tips for Coordinating with Teammates in CS

Qijie
2023-11-24
Life & Misc
CS, Teamwork

There are several kinds of teammates

First, the normal ones

These depend on skill level. If they’re stronger than you, you can follow up on their frags or pull enemy fire for them. If you’re well coordinated, you can help set up a multi-person hold (use it as little as possible, except for the fast A-short push on Dust II), as well as the “cheese sandwich” (crossfire, the deadliest of all).

Take Inferno as an example, as in the picture: as a CT, you stand in the red box position and your teammate stands in the blue box position — that’s the best way to hold A1. Which is also why Inferno CTs are recommended to hold 3A 2B; other angle setups work the same way. Of course, whether the link needs attention depends on the situation — don’t hold one angle rigidly.

One more example and one more picture:

As CT, the big mid area will probably get smoked off by the Ts like this. At that point you can try a double boost with a teammate to watch the T exit, and trade some damage while you’re at it. Remember: one look down mid is fine; take a second look and (you) will “explode”.

Some teammates start to get pushy

When you run into this kind of rager, remember: don’t bicker back, don’t let it get to you mentally (just mute them), and play your own game in peace.

All the verbal abuse in the world is complete nonsense in the face of real skill!

For example: when fighting over Inferno’s A2, have him go in first as the entry and pull fire, as in the picture:

As CT, holding the second angle here, holding it directly, and even pushing it as a T are all dangerous (especially when the enemy has a shotgun). So you can tell the rager to go first (there’s a good chance he won’t listen and will curse you out instead, but while he’s being impulsive you can finish off two of them — isn’t that worth it?), then follow behind him and trade. Remember, this counts as a good technique for playing CS with at most one rager. That way his score will come down like a swoosh (most likely), while you and the rest of the team end up indirectly coordinating with him — damn, that feels good!

Can’t do anything about him? Try not giving him a gun for a few rounds in a row (a low-skill big-mouth usually doesn’t have great economy anyway) — pay him back in his own coin. (Use this as little as possible; there’s a risk of getting kicked.)

Make sure you can bring out your real skill, and let your play do the talking.

Note: the 2023-11-08 update added the rule that solo players can’t be kicked, so that a four-stack of ragers can’t kick a normal solo player at the drop of a hat and leave the solo player with no game experience at all.

When the rager starts harassing you with molotovs/grenades or keeps “accidentally” damaging teammates, you can hold Tab, right-click, click his avatar and hit the triangle exclamation mark (report). There’s a small chance he gets a non-permanent ban.

Essentially, a rager is just someone venting all the anger they’ve built up in daily life onto their teammates in a PvP game, and most of them are low-skill big-mouths. Of course, a small fraction are high-skill players who still pressure their teammates; when you meet one of these high-skill ragers, it’s best to let him play alone as much as possible — maybe he’ll curse his way into holding or breaking a bomb site.

Sometimes a teammate even starts spinning in circles AFK at spawn

At that point you can use him as bait: hold an angle on him with a holding gun like the Negev. The other side will at least be curious why there’s one fewer of us over there, so they’ll come and knife the AFK guy (high chance they come running with a knife). And then, hehe! You know what I mean.

A teammate starts cheating

This match loses whatever meaning it had. Kick him if you can; if you can’t, just play it loose. The game usually devolves into HvH.

Better report him, so the cheating dog doesn’t end up on the other side next game and disgust you. Don’t rush into the next match after it ends — take a breather, treat it as a walk to clear your head.

Finally

In a round of CS, teammates are consumables: every one the enemy picks off is one fewer you have, so make the most of this precious teammate resource.

Anyway, I have one principle when playing CS: each week I only play until I level up and I’m nearly full on XP, then I stop (except for doing operation missions). Stay away from ragers — a smile keeps you ten years younger.

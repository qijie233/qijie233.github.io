A Detailed Look at CS Competitive Mode Mechanics

Qijie
2023-09-20
CSGO

This article mainly covers the game mechanics of Competitive mode. Since Hostage mode has few players, I won’t cover it here.

In CS2, Competitive mode shortens matches to 13 wins with overtime that can drag on for 6 rounds, but it’s still a 5v5 mode the whole way.

Economy

See the thing in the top-left corner of the screen (in CS2, the bottom-left corner)

, right? That’s your in-game money.

It’s used to buy weapons and equipment for use in the match.

In Competitive mode, the starting money is $800.

In the pistol round (the first round, and the first round after switching sides), you can pick one of the following options:

  • Half armor $650 Recommended for beginners, it saves you from getting dropped in a few shots by opponents who can't hit heads. Though against someone who can hit heads, it's still a one-tap to the head.
  • Tec9 or FN57 $500 (What I usually buy) An aggressive choice. The Tec9 has the best running stability (no wonder it's the SMG among pistols), and the FN57 has a 20-round magazine; if you can handle these two guns, they'll put enormous pressure on the other side.
  • Desert Eagle $700 Strongly not recommended, because this pistol demands even better counter-strafing than other guns.
  • Defuse kit $400 (CT only) Well, actually only a minority of players buy just a defuse kit in the pistol round.

If you have a team mindset, you can buy a few more grenades and play off each other.

Money-Earning Mechanics

Here are the kill rewards:

Weapon type Reward per kill
Pistols, rifles (including the scout and autosnipers), machine guns, P90 +$300
AWP +$100
SMGs (except the P90) +$600
Shotguns +$900
(Heh heh, let me try) knife! +$1500
“Ahh~” Zeus x27, “The Camper’s Happy Gun” +$0 (i.e. nothing)

Accidentally killing a teammate out of carelessness costs -$300 (in official matchmaking).

Here’s the losing-streak money mechanic:

If T drags it out until time runs out, that’s +$0~

Compensation mechanic (drops a notch after a win) What you get on a loss
0 notches +$1400
1 notch +$1900
2 notches (both sides are here in the pistol round) +$2400
3 notches +$2900
4 notches +$3400

Here’s the win reward mechanic:

Achieving the objective: +$3500, plus an extra +$300 for the person who completes it

Note: for T, the extra reward for the planter is settled immediately after the bomb is planted.

Wiping out the enemy: +$3250

Money Allocation

Now that you understand these money mechanics, it’s worth making some plans.

After winning the pistol round, consider
CT/T: buying full armor + MP7/Nova/XM1014

and a few grenades to coordinate, extending your advantage after the win.

Of course, when your opponents are on a lucky streak, you might lose the pistol round. In that case consider:

T: Full armor (because CT’s M4 needs two shots to take out a fully armored head, so you get a bit more margin for error) + MAC10 (the hairdryer)/Nova/XM1014/even Tec9

CT: Half armor (AKs and other rifles one-tap the head anyway, so don’t waste money on a helmet ($350)) + Negev/MP9 (little bee)/Nova/MAG-7/XM1014/even FN57

I have to say, the autoshotgun really is a cure-all. Though don’t use it when your aim and rhythm are off.

(Not recommended for beginners) Of course you can gamble: no armor and a scout, take a shot or two mid and run; with luck you can even trade the scout for a rifle.

You can also buy nothing and full-buy a rifle next round (ECO).

The $4750 AWP is for maintaining an advantage in the late game, not for making a bang and being used as a telescope. The AWP is best placed in the hands of a teammate who can actually snipe, since the AWP has a high ceiling and a very low floor. So: on T side you want at most one AWP, while on CT side it’s best to have at least two.

Bomb Defusal Mode

A more classic mode. Most maps are divided into A/B bomb sites: T needs to plant the bomb and wait for it to explode, or wipe out the enemy, while CT needs to defuse or wipe out the enemy. In official matchmaking, the bomb explodes 40s after planting, and the blast radius is large (on de_inferno — “Shut-in Town” — the bomb damage at A site was increased, giving anyone crouching in the corner of the A pit a 90% chance of being blown up).

In this mode, some people camp in corners to ambush, some push out to retake and cover the rear, and someone even pulled off a “Mute Camper” ninja defuse around a single corner (hard to do in CS2, since smoke can be blown apart by grenades)…

So you need to know your role.

For example, I mostly play lurk/refrag, sometimes entry, and even a few rounds of AWP when my aim is on.

Here are the roles in the general sense:

  • Refrag/lurk: has some ability to close out clutches, usually holds the second angle (so the enemy won’t spot your position the moment they come around), and covers positions your teammates aren’t watching (that is, the camper);
  • Entry fragger: superb aim, always taking the first duel, relatively aggressive;
  • AWPer: fast reactions; give him an AWP and he can create and sustain an advantage;
  • Support: deep understanding of the map, great at throwing utility to create advantages;
  • IGL: as the name suggests, the guy who calls which site to hit and when to rotate;
  • Flex: covers all of the above and can switch roles quickly.

Do you know yourself? Are you ready to face opponents on your level (matchmaking may pair you with opponents somewhat stronger than you)?

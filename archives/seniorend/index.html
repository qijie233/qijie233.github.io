Maybe a Happy End to These Three Years?

Qijie
2025-06-26
Life & Misc › Reflections
Daily Life, Rant, Recap, Society

The blogger has graduated from high school. I just picked up my diploma, and for some reason I feel kind of low.

I went the school's early independent-recruitment route instead. I wasn't in the honors class myself, so I took the early admission, and got into a college in Suzhou in the first round. Taking the gaokao would have needed 460+, right at the second-tier cutoff, and I might not have hit that line anyway, so going the early admission route was honestly a bit of a relief. When I went to that Suzhou college for the exam, it was 100 points for the interview plus 100 for Chinese and math, 200 in total. My math is terrible, but my interview made up for that nicely: 93.6 out of 100, which I'd guess puts me at the top of the school.

Thinking it over, my grades across those three years were never really good. I wasn't at a key high school, and I wasn't in the honors class either. Most of my interest every day went into tinkering: tinkering with computers, tinkering with servers, tinkering with websites, tinkering with open-source programs. In my first year I hoped to get into a decent university; in my second year I wanted to test into Nanjing Railway Vocational and Technical College; by my third year, when it came time to actually choose, I found even that school was hard for me, the competition was just too fierce. My third year was a 45-degree life: not flat enough to lie down, not upright enough to stand up. At that Suzhou college I put mechanical engineering as my first choice and wasn't admitted; software technology as my second choice, also not admitted; Internet of Things as my third choice, admitted. Felt like such a failure. I even picked up a case of depression in high school and hardly took any medication; I was kind of averse to it. I don't want to remember that I'm ill; I work hard to make myself look like I don't have that many problems, and honestly that's tiring. I got backstabbed by friends in high school too, and it'd be a lie to say that didn't hurt. Over those three years I had no really good friends either; fitting into other people's circles felt really hard........ and tiring too. I can chat a couple of lines with anyone and greet everyone with a smile, which is exhausting. Doing that makes me look like I'm well-liked, and I guess I am, sort of: over three years of high school I made no enemies.

To some people in my grade, I might have been a "big name around school" those three years. I was the school's host: all the grade-level events were hosted by me, and big school-wide events too, like the New Year's performance, the sports meet, guided tours at the school museum, the coming-of-age ceremony, out-of-school study trips and so on. People who don't know me probably wouldn't guess what goes on in this little head of mine.

Three years in, I don't know whether anyone liked me, but here, in a place almost nobody sees, I'll open up: there's someone I liked. It's all very clichéd anyway.

Collapsed (in a whisper)

Since I've written it all out anyway, don't laugh at me, whoever sees this. One of these days, if I have the time, I'll adapt it into a short story.

Let's call the girl Xiaoyu. We've known each other since second grade, though we weren't at the same primary school. Bet you didn't see that coming. We went to the same middle school too, and after high school started, when we split into subject groups, we ended up in the same class. And that was that........ though we barely had any contact in middle school either. She dated someone for over a year from first to second year of high school, then they broke up, and she started coming to chat with me at night. I'm a person who basically has no one to chat with at night; I just shitpost in group chats, dozens of groups, a bit every day. If Xiaoyu hadn't asked me, I'd never have noticed that I really did have nobody to talk to at night. Back then I always figured it was no big deal.

But honestly, chat with someone long enough and feelings really do creep in. Just around then she'd had a fight with a girl in our class, so I quietly played the role of a hollow tree, slowly listening and slowly comforting. Okay, maybe I actually do have a bit of a gift for this (lol, not really). Like I said above, chat enough and you really do start to feel something for her. By the time I realized I liked Xiaoyu, I felt it was already too late; there's really no easy way to just decide not to like her. I wanted to get closer to her, step by step. Mm....... to be honest I had no experience, and at first nobody in the class had any either. After class I'd just like to look at her (not a hentai). Looking back now, I really was so clumsy. Mm......... holy crap, I actually did go and confess. Holy crap, holy crap, holy crap, I don't even know how I dared to (😱). And then I got rejected hahahahahahahaha fuck. No harm done, it was within my expectations, but our relationship was that ambiguous, neither-here-nor-there kind, which was explosive..........

Then, after agonizing over it for months, I decided to give up. Honestly, that stretch was hell mode for me (😭)

Good news: I got over her.

Bad news: she confessed to me.

Worse news: I turned her down.

Fate played an enormous joke on me, and a well-played one too, and then she stopped talking to me (2024.6.19).

But lately we're on good terms again, and I don't really mind anymore. Maybe I still like her? Her reason for turning me down back then was that she couldn't like anyone anymore, but honestly I'm pretty tired now too (collapses).

But as those three years drew to a close, my heart was still a little heavy. I walked out of the school alone, went home with a classmate who lives nearby, and thought about a lot on the way. My chest felt a bit stuffy. I knew it clearly: the story of my high school, the one that belonged to me, was over. So much happened in those three years, so much really did happen.

No matter how thick a book is, there comes a time when you turn to its last page; no matter how unforgettable a story is, there comes a time when it ends. With so much that's unforgettable and so much I can't bear to leave behind, standing at the fork to the next stop full of hesitation and nostalgia, but rather than standing there thinking it over, it's better to walk forward one step at a time.

The hands of a clock go round and round, just like a person's confusion, worries and weakness swinging endlessly, but in the end people still have to move forward, just as a clock's hands always end up pointing forward.

And then, toward tomorrow.

I've posted the line below I don't know how many times. Today, let me give it to myself.

That's a wrap, confetti and all. Thanks for keeping me company.

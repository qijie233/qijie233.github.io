2025 Year-End Recap

Qijie
2025-12-26
Life & Misc › Reflections
Daily Life, Recap

It's that time of year again — time for the annual year-end recap. Let Qijie think about what all has happened this year~

Looking Back

The Website

The Blog

I published eight posts in total this year, which is quite few — not as many as before (lazy), or maybe my urge to express myself has dropped a bit

Let's have a look at the Bing site report

The click count really is huge, and it honestly feels unbelievable to me. Next, let's look at the site statistics

This is this year's blog traffic record. It isn't what you'd call large in scale, but I suppose I do have a little place of my own on the internet. Compared with last year, though, the numbers show a slight downward trend — not by much. All in all, this year didn't take things to the next level.

The most-clicked post on the blog this year was the NetEase Cloud Music API&MetingApi one, so I guess I've become an internet philanthropist of sorts

New Additions

https://163api.qijieya.cn/To be honest, I originally set this up for my QQ bot to parse the NetEase Cloud Music share links/cards people post in groups, but then that feature kept breaking and I couldn't be bothered to fix it, so I just dropped it altogether. The API site is still sitting there, though, and I've hardly ever advertised that it exists — I only mentioned it in my own site list — yet the number of calls this API site gets is terrifying. The overall stats will come later.

I made some small changes to https://api.qijieya.cn/meting and added a statistics feature. According to the records, it gets called about 10k times a day — the main reason is probably that I have a NetEase Cloud Music VIP

Let's look at the calls to the two API sites over the last 30 days. The last 30 days is because I only migrated the API sites to Alibaba Cloud ESA at the end of the year — otherwise the origin server couldn't take it anymore, and other CDNs are too expensive to afford. Special thanks here to Alibaba Cloud ESA for providing free, unlimited CDN.

Tens of millions of calls really does take too much out of the servers. Thanks again, Alibaba Cloud

Added registration and usage access for the Qijie image host — every registered user can use the free, high-speed Qijie image host

Added a few new domains. I don't even know why — I saw that six-digit xyz domains were cheap and just bought ten years of one? Aaaah, I can't do this again next time

Added the email service for the lty.today domain

Added the email service for the lty.edu.kg domain

Filed ICP registration for a few more domains

Removals & Changes

Switched the site's CDN across the board to Volcano Engine, except for the API sites. There were two main reasons: one, it's cheap; two, HTTPS requests aren't charged. But during the time I've been using it, the price went up — though within a range I can accept, and it's now a bit more expensive than Huawei Cloud's CDN. One thing worth pointing out is that Volcano Engine's ticket replies are especially slow.

The "spy on Qijie's website" project I ran for less than a month has been shut down. The main reason was that the client side was a hassle; the server side was fine.

Redesigned the homepage style of https://www.qijieya.cn

Stopped exposing UPtimekuma to the public internet and switched to Qijie's Status Duck - Komari Monitor

https://github.com/komari-monitor/komari, and the Nezha probe is no longer in use either

Stopped using 51.LA site analytics — this will be my last year using 51's site stats — and switched to https://umami.is/ . Thanks here to BA4SAK for providing the Umami instance. There are two reasons I don't host it myself: one is that I don't have a server with spare capacity to set it up on, and the other, more important reason is that I'm lazy. Yeah

Shut down the public Alist site. The main reason isn't that the Alist project blew up, but that using it over the public internet isn't very convenient, and because the server bandwidth is small, uploads are somewhat limited. I'm using openlist on the home NAS now

Shut down the CDN site. The biggest reason is that I no longer use servers to distribute static assets and have switched to Alibaba Cloud OSS

Servers

https://server.qijieya.cn/

This page lets you see every server I currently have in use

This year I added two Tencent Cloud servers and had the ICP registration done through Tencent Cloud

I have two Tencent Cloud accounts, one company account and one personal account, and both accounts have two identical-spec Shanghai servers

Alibaba Cloud also has two accounts, likewise one company and one personal. The company account has an Alibaba Cloud "99 Plan" Hangzhou server that's been running for several years now, and the environment inside it has become a total mess, but I have to say this server still carries the vast majority of my websites; Alibaba Cloud also has a lightweight cloud server with peak 200 Mbps bandwidth. The main reason I went with Alibaba rather than Tencent is that I have a few no-threshold 100-yuan coupons on Alibaba Cloud

The home cloud is still a NAS I put together myself. I originally had the idea of buying a Dell server, but because memory prices went up recently I had to give up on it — the main reason being that the NAS I built myself has rather poor expandability, and the drives are running a little short. But the home cloud's broadband was switched from China Mobile to China Unicom and I got a public IP, otherwise having only public IPv6 would still be a bit of a hassle.

About Me

What is there for me to look back on this year

This year I got into university in Shanghai (struck out) Taicang, Suzhou. My school is only a bit over 40 km from Shanghai Hongqiao Airport — going to Shanghai is quite convenient, going to Suzhou isn't）.

At the beginning of the year I registered a company, and later swapped company shares with a friend, so I'm a shareholder in his company too. I won't say the names of the two companies. My own company has taken on a few small jobs.

Went to the Google Developer Conference and AOSCC, and learned a bit of new technology.

Went to BilibliWorld, which has pretty much become a must-go for me every year. I didn't manage to grab a ticket this year, so I spent several hundred yuan on an invitation-only ticket. This year I also went to ComicCup (China's biggest doujin convention) — CP31 in May, and CPSP over the National Day holiday. One thing worth mentioning: a girl I became friends with at BW (her WeChat was on the merch she handed out) — I ran into her again at CPSP during the National Day holiday. I always go to conventions by myself, and rarely have anyone to go with, and the most unexpected thing is that the city my university is in is actually the same city as her home — that's what I find the most incredible coincidence.

Going to conventions alone can sometimes feel really painful inside, because it's always just me. Seeing other people together makes me quite envious. Maybe I'm just not very good at socializing. But then why go? To see the IPs I like standing right in front of me in another, tangible form — that's the part that makes me happiest. I'm the kind of person who crawls around dark and twisted almost never dares to approach people and add them as friends (😭)

This year I fell into the amateur radio rabbit hole and met a lot of new friends. The thing I regret least this year is getting into amateur radio — I really did meet so many friends. My offline social circle is normally very small (though I really am trying to expand it, but some reasons of my own make it difficult), so most of my socializing happens online. QQ is what I use the most; I have 95 group chats on QQ. Yep, that's right — ninety-five.

Amateur radio moved that part of my social life from online to offline. I've been to several offline events: I came over from Nanjing, went to two offline events held by the provincial association in Nanjing, and went to two offline events in Kunshan. Special thanks here to BA4SGR in Kunshan — maybe he'll never see this article, but he's the one who brought me into the Kunshan amateur radio association. It felt a bit like a parent taking their kid along to visit relatives (aaah, what a weird metaphor). Getting "Outstanding Net Control" at this year's Kunshan amateur radio annual meeting is also very much thanks to him

Any regrets? Yes, I do have some. A teacher recommended me to another teacher to take part in an FPGA competition, and I stayed in the group grinding away for nearly two weeks. At first the teacher said it was easy, but after all it was a programming language plus a domain I'd never touched before, so I learned extremely slowly. The teacher said it was simpler than STM32, but once I actually got my hands on it I found it far harder than STM32. I got the hang of STM32 in one evening, but with FPGA it took me a whole week just to find the door. The senior in the group wasn't willing to talk to me either — maybe because of the competitive relationship, and he had no obligation to mentor me anyway. There weren't any fairly systematic tutorials online either. In the end I quit. To this day I still don't know whether that was the right choice.

The most unfortunate thing is that at the end of the year I was diagnosed with moderate depression and bipolar disorder. Though I can't really call it unfortunate — this time last year the doctor told me to take medication and I didn't, because I didn't want to, because I didn't want to believe I was sick. Only recently, when it really got unbearable, did I go to the hospital. I can't stand myself. Haha, to other people it's impossible that I'd be depressed, because I'm smiling in front of everyone, trying hard to be on good terms with every single person.

Looking Ahead

What should I do next year? I can't really explain clearly right now, but I still managed to think up a few things

Travel to Japan

Improve the website (in the broad sense)

Open a bank card in Hong Kong

Make the commercial project I'm developing with friends stronger

Post a few more blog updates (I really posted so few this year)

Alright, that's all I'll write. Here's wishing myself a happy new year

And last of all

Here's to the imperfect... tomorrow.

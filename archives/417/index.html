A Look at Some Phone 'Features' and How to Deal With Them

Qijie
2023-10-13
Reflections › Tech Notes
Android, Features, Linux, Phone

Preface

Phones are now inseparable from every part of our lives. Yet not a single app that comes bundled with the thing (especially from the big brands) sits still. At the mild end, you get three or four pointless system app notifications a day; at the severe end, while you’re reading a page or scrolling videos, something goes “Biu~” and an ad pops up that you can’t close for a while — sometimes even with a fake close button. When you get annoyed and don’t want to accept such “blessings”, the system says: “System app, cannot be uninstalled.”

I won’t name names here — you all know who they are.

Now let’s list the common “features”!

Features!

System app notifications

Mainly shows up as:

  • A fixed 3+ notifications a day
  • Accompanied by notification sounds that interrupt you
  • Mostly content with no actual meaning

This can be turned off in system settings. However, after you manually kill the notifications from the “App Store” on the Blue Factory (that is, _ _ _ _ 50, fill in the blanks yourself), it will “thoughtfully” turn them back on for you, completely unnoticed. (So far I’ve had one report of this behavior.)

Quick Apps

Mainly shows up as:

  • While you’re browsing a page, chatting, or scrolling videos, it does a “Jump Scare” right into your face (full screen)
  • The back button may be disabled
  • It jumps around like crazy, with some disgusting content along the way
  • It downloads and installs apps automatically (this one is brutal)

You can also search for Quick Apps in system settings to turn off the related “features”. Before rooting, you can’t uninstall or disable it.

It doesn’t matter whether it’s the Blue Factory or the Green Factory ( _ _ _ _ figure it out yourself) — anything tied to domestic brands has it.

Preinstalled apps from the start

These, well, you can uninstall them manually.

But there’s a folder sitting quietly on the home screen called “App Recommendations” that can’t be deleted — you can only turn it off in system settings.

This is about the friendliest one at the moment, though there are ones that install the moment you tap them.

Uploading data in the background

Mainly shows up as:

  • When you’re not using the phone (with a perfectly clean background — yeah right), you check the phone’s throughput on the router admin page and find the upload bandwidth is never 0KB/s
  • After you take a photo, the gallery automatically uploads it to the corresponding cloud
  • Besides the system uploading logs that have nothing to do with you and aren’t sensitive, some brands go all the way and won’t even spare your contacts
  • On PC, one particular chat app is a standout — the kind that scans your whole disk

Regular people really can’t tell this is happening, and it’s the scariest one right now. Many people have sensitive things in their galleries — ID card photos, bank card photos, even household register photos — and it all gets uploaded without a second thought. Which makes illegal doxxing a bit easier (don’t get any bad ideas).

Surveillance and eavesdropping in the background

Mainly shows up as:

  • The microphone and camera turn on by themselves
  • The file manager can’t find the recorded files (most likely)
  • Accompanied by automatic uploading (less likely)
  • For third-party apps, they request camera and microphone permissions and (some of them) crash immediately if you refuse

This one is about as harmful as the previous “feature”. Just be careful not to hand out permissions carelessly and cover the camera at the right time.

Waking each other up in the background

Mainly shows up as:

  • A chat app wakes up a shopping app with no human involvement…
  • One wakes another, that one wakes yet another, and your battery is gone in seconds
  • Hard to stop

The reason this happens: apps abroad have a unified notification mechanism in Google’s FCM, which greatly reduces how often apps wake each other. In China, though, there’s no unified notification control mechanism, so once an app is killed in the background and needs to deliver a notification, the apps wake each other up in the background.

Setting Developer options can fix it, but that’s too extreme.

Auto-downloading and installing apps on certain actions

Commonly known as “bundling”.

Mainly shows up as:

  • P2P High-Speed Downloader (the Windows one), XX Battery Saver, XX Cleanup Master
  • Security App Recommendations and the like, pre-checked for you
  • One gives birth to two, two to three, three to ten thousand things. And then those ten thousand things blow up your phone, PC, and whatever else

You really can’t guard against this. Remember: just keep a bit of your wits about you, and don’t touch anything that isn’t from the official site.

This is where package managers like apt show their worth — unified management (on desktop Linux) sidesteps the bundling that comes from non-official sources.

“Your browser version is too old, please update to the latest browser”

Mainly shows up as:

  • Happens often while browsing
  • Downloads a browser automatically (nothing but a Chromium wrapper) and throws in a backdoor for free
  • Very likely accompanied by other “features”

Honestly, for browsers Chromium/Edge (Chromium, but with a few little Microsoft tweaks)/Firefox is all you need; there’s no reason to use any of the other assorted junk.

App splash screen ads

Mainly shows up as:

  • An ad pops up automatically when you launch the app
  • The skip button is tiny, so it’s easy to tap the ad by mistake
  • Some even open the ad with a “shake”, and it’s extremely sensitive

This one is pretty gross. The slightest movement and you’re off to another page. And it never really gets banned.

Breaking the “Features”, Chasing “Ego”

Getting to know the basics: Root, the bootloader lock, and friends

Root

Coming from the Unix era, running through Linux, and even reaching Android (a heavily modified Linux, though it gets kicked out of the Linux distro club on a daily basis),

it has always been the highest-privilege user that can do Anything.

No-brain rm -rf / newbies are not welcome here.

Be careful before using root privileges — as the first-time sudo message puts it:

Here it’d be best to replace “typing” with “doing”.

Bootloader lock

A phone starts booting from the bootloader (just like a PC’s UEFI, except it can also boot Linux).

But the bootloader lock means that, while locked, the phone can’t modify system partitions such as boot, system, and so on.

At first this thing had good intentions: it makes it impossible to flash root packages like Magisk, preventing clueless newbies from bricking their phones by poking around.

But the fact that root lets users uninstall the built-in “blessing” apps (and not only those) thoroughly enraged a few people.

So they decided to lock root away on phones and fob us off with excuses. (Total Genshin main quest chapter 3 déjà vu)

Far ahead!

Flashing packages

These packages can change system files. For example, Magisk can replace files in boot so that the system gains root privileges.

Before flashing, verify the SHA256 to make sure nothing goes wrong.

Once you know these basics, you can roll up your sleeves and try it (and, with some probability, “pass away”).

Author's note: tinkering carries risk, root with caution.
Flash carelessly, and you'll shed two lines of tears over a brick.

Starting with buying the device

First, avoid the following brands, because they’ve already locked bootloader unlocking on most models:

  • Blue Factory, Green Factory
  • the one that’s forever “far ahead!”
  • Xiaomi (high bar)

Then choose based on how hard it is to find flashable packages, value for money, how easy the system is to modify, and how much unlocking breaks the original features.

For example: “the one that did four Genshin collabs (as of 2023-10-13)”, “Elysia’s signet”, Nexus, and so on.

Buying the device is where the bootloader-unlocking story begins.

Unlocking the bootloader

You can look up the unlock guide on the official site, XDA, and so on. I won’t ramble here.

Some models unlock easily — one command does it: fastboot oem unlock,

On some models the manufacturer strangles the process.

The most puzzling part here: unlocking the bootloader wipes user data. If it’s a phone you’ve already owned and used for a while, back up your data first.

Flashing the root package

You can find the Magisk package for your device on a search engine or on XDA.

For the official Magisk package, extract boot.img yourself and patch it. (Recommended)

(Optional) Flashing LSPosed

Installed Magisk and still want to mod the system more deeply?

LSPosed (the “old pervert” module — no idea where that came from) can do it!

Changing app behavior and system operations all depends on it.

Slimming down the system

You can use adb to disable the apps that come with the system (a bit tedious). For example: ban the background services and apps that come with “blessings”, and shut the smart assistant up while you’re at it…

You can be lazy here: just search for “ 搞机助手·R “ (as long as you don’t grab an unripe version from some download site).

You can also look up a freezing list for your brand and copy the homework. (Not recommended)

Walk by the river often enough and your shoes will get wet

When you’ve added a module or banned a system app and are about to reboot, you may end up in an endless reboot loop, or in Fastboot.

I strongly recommend flashing one more thing: a volume-key Magisk rescue module.

Overclocking the phone!

Note: this part may risk damaging the device!

When the phone’s performance doesn’t satisfy you, you can get extra performance out of it for free by beating the thermal control and applying high-intensity scheduling.

You can even overclock it.

Search the corresponding keywords on a search engine and you’ll find the resources.

In closing

Maybe the features built into the phone system do outweigh what root gives you, but to hold the phone more firmly in your own hands and stop putting up with the “blessings”, rooting beats not rooting.

Root is dead?

Root is alive.

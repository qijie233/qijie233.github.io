A Few eSIMs I've Been Looking Into Recently

Qijie
2025-12-24
Life & Misc › Tech Notes
Phone, eSIM

I've always used giffgaff as my overseas phone card, and recently I got recommended Hong Kong 🇭🇰's https://www.clubsim.com.hk/zh/, but it only supports eSIM, so I started looking into eSIM. The most important point: my card slots are already full — one main phone card, one data card, one campus card, and one giffgaff

eSIM card or eSIM phone

At first my friend recommended that I use an eSIM phone, like a Pixel or a US-version iPhone and so on (domestic eSIMs can't be written to foreign-version phones; you can look up the details yourself), because he told me there would be a few issues, but not much impact (I'll talk about those later). I also have a spare phone, and at the time I was just thinking of buying it to play around with, so I directly bought an eUICC white card on Pinduoduo

Then there's also branded eSIM cards like 9esim, Estk and such, and that's because... they're expensive. Yep, that's exactly how I felt at the time. Anyway, I just bought one to try it out, and I never expected it would end up being my main card. Let's see how Gemini rates the difference between the two kinds of cards

Genimi's answer

In the field of eSIM technology, eUICC white card (eUICC White Card) is usually the umbrella term for this kind of product, while 9eSIM is one specific commercial brand product among them.

Simply put, their relationship is like the difference between **"blank cassette tapes" (eUICC white cards) and "branded cassette tapes with management features" (9eSIM)**.

Here is a detailed comparison of the two:

1. Definition and positioning

  • eUICC white card (Generic eUICC Card):

    • Definition: refers to programmable blank SIM card hardware that complies with the GSMA standard. It is essentially a carrier with an eUICC chip inside.

    • Source: usually a bare card straight from the factory or a test card (Test Card) aimed at developers, with no specific brand packaging, or with very casual OEM branding.

    • Positioning: mainly aimed at developers, geeks, or users who know eSIM technology very well. The user has to fiddle with the writing software themselves.

  • 9eSIM (and similar competitors such as 5ber, eSTK.me):

    • Definition: a consumer-grade product based on eUICC white card hardware, packaged with dedicated management software (App) and firmware optimizations.

    • Positioning: aimed at ordinary mass-market consumers, especially users who need to travel abroad and want to use eSIM without changing phones.

    • Core value: what's being sold is "service" and "ease of use", not just the card itself.

2. Differences in features and experience (the core differences)

Feature

eUICC white card (generic/universal version)

9eSIM (taking the latest V3 version as an example)

Bundled app

Usually no dedicated app; mainly relies on open-source software (such as OpenEUICC, EasyEUICC) or PC-side card reader software.

Provides an official app (Android), with a friendlier interface; the workflow (scan a code to download an eSIM) has been optimized, so even beginners can get the hang of it.

iPhone support

Extremely troublesome. Usually you can't switch numbers directly on an iPhone; you have to insert it into an Android phone or a card reader, write the number first, then put it back into the iPhone.

Supports the STK menu. The card comes with a simple built-in menu, so iPhone users can switch numbers directly in "Settings - Cellular - SIM Card Applications", with no need for an Android phone.

Write limits

Some older white card versions (such as V0) may have poor compatibility, and may even have a write-count limit or the risk of getting locked up.

Officially claimed to support unlimited erase and write cycles, and the V3 version has better compatibility with the new eSIM provisioning protocols.

Storage capacity

All over the place, usually small (a few hundred KB), and the numbers it can store are limited.

The V3 version claims about 1.5MB of space, theoretically enough for 50+ eSIM profiles.

After-sales support

Basically none; you need a certain amount of coding/debugging knowledge to solve errors by yourself.

Has official firmware updates (OTA) and customer support; if a particular carrier's profile won't download, they may provide a fix.

3. Price differences

  • eUICC white card: extremely cheap, usually around 20-50 RMB (commonly seen in Xianyu or Taobao DIY shops).

  • 9eSIM card: pricier, usually 100-200 RMB (about $20-$30 USD), which covers the hardware premium and the software development cost.

4. Buying advice

  • When to pick an "eUICC white card":

    • You're a tech geek and have an Android phone such as a Pixel that supports open-source eSIM frameworks.

    • You have a PC/SC card reader and are used to managing cards on a computer.

    • Your budget is very limited, and you only plan to store one or two numbers for fixed use without switching often.

  • When to pick "9eSIM" (or 5ber/eSTK.me):

    • You're an iPhone user (this is the most critical one, because you need the STK menu to switch numbers).

    • You don't want to deal with complicated configuration and just want to scan a code and use it.

    • You travel abroad often and need to store dozens of eSIMs from different countries in the card, ready to switch at any time.

    • You need reliable after-sales support, so that if the card breaks or won't accept a profile, there's someone to ask.

Summary: 9eSIM is in fact just an "advanced eUICC white card" with optimized firmware, a built-in STK menu, and a foolproof companion app. If you're not in it to dig into the technology, but only for convenience when going abroad, I'd suggest spending a bit more on 9eSIM or a similar branded card — it saves you a lot of compatibility trouble.

Honestly, I really do want to switch to this kind of eSIM. It's a bit pricey, but honestly the features aren't bad. The thing is, switching to 9esim is a bit hard for me right now — as for why, I'll get to it later when I talk about eSIM carriers. Worth mentioning: these ordinary white cards don't limit the number of erases and writes either

In short, what I'm using right now is an ordinary eUICC card together with two apps, EasyEuicc and 9eSIM-NLPA, for writing and switching profiles

eSIM experience on Android

Since I'm currently dual-wielding Android phones, it's very worth mentioning here that NekokoLPA may freeze after switching a card — it can only stay on the card it just switched to, and won't read the rest. You have to pull the card and reinsert it or restart the phone, so the experience isn't great, if only it supported STK card switching, it would be perfect (dream on)

The latest report as of writing!! The card I bought supports self-service card switching right inside the SIM card application, with no need for external software! Filler A blessing in disguise for the post, I suppose, but it's still worth noting that the SIM card application can't read the card, and you still have to restart the phone to do things like writing a profile.

This is the biggest problem with these novice white cards. Big brands like 9eSIM may have optimizations for this issue, but being that much more expensive, Qijie still feels it's a bit not worth it

The eSIMs I'm currently using

ClubSim

https://www.clubsim.com.hk/zh/

CSL, being Hong Kong's number one (self-proclaimed) carrier, has a very good deal on keeping your number with its ClubSIM prepaid card — you only need to buy a plan of at least 6 HKD each year to keep the number

If you don't have a ClubSIM yet, you need to buy a SIM/eSIM card in advance; and if you open an eSIM, you need to add a plan of no less than $50 to the card, so the first year comes to 56 HKD.

Real-name registration requires a passport

Tip: ClubSIM allows at most one eSIM change per year; if you go over that or lose the eSIM card, you need to go to a physical store to replace it with a new eSIM card

China-HK Unicom Cuniq

https://www.cuniq.com/

This is my main one right now, and I really, really like it

Because this card is dual-number in one card: one Hong Kong +852 number and one mainland +86 number. The first month is 48 HKD, and afterwards you can switch to the 9 HKD number-keeping plan. I think that price is really good — a number-keeping plan for a single domestic number is already eight yuan, and this is only 9 HKD for two numbers

I won't go into the details of how to get the card; you can find them on https://forum.naixi.net/ and X, so check for yourself if you're interested. What I want to remind you of here is:

  • The mainland +86 phone number needs real-name registration with an ID card

  • After activating dual-number-in-one-card, when you call a mainland number from the mainland, the other side sees your mainland number, with the region shown as Shenzhen, Guangdong — but the other person's phone will flag you as an "overseas call"

  • Replacing the eSIM card must be done at a local Unicom store in Hong Kong

This card is also fairly cheap for calls, only 0.3 HKD/minute — you could say it's a pretty all-round card. Later, if you want data, you can top up on the China-HK Unicom website; the prices are 20 HKD/1GB and 50 HKD/5GB. The price is middling — it's roaming data after all, so don't expect it to be cheap (actually the price is about the same as domestic)

GiffGaff

https://www.giffgaff.com/

I'll skip this one — it counts as a fairly popular magic card in China for keeping numbers and receiving SMS codes, though it has currently stopped mailing physical cards to China. eSIM still works fine; if you need to convert a physical card to eSIM (which is what Qijie did), you'll need this website https://esim.kim/giffgaff/, and after the conversion it gives you a QR code, which you still scan to import the eSIM

German Vodafone

https://www.vodafone.de/

This number needs a passport for real-name registration, and you need to top up once every three months. The official site has a minimum top-up of five euros, so the cost is on the high side — but you can top it up by transferring 1 euro from a Wise euro account

The detailed how-to guide is linked herehow-to guide

RedTeaGO (Red Tea Mobile)

No phone number, purely a data card

China data is 100G/365 days/49.9 USD. That's pretty much the price everywhere and falls within the acceptable range. There are some other plans you can check on the app yourself

You can all use my invite code (quietly) MOEQ0001 — after you spend some money you get a 3 USD balance reward

They Deserve the Respect They Are Due

Qijie
2023-10-17
Qijie's Ramblings › Reflections
Economy, Society

MTF

MtF (Male-to-Female) refers to transgender women — women whose sex assigned at birth was male. It’s also called gender identity disorder. About this group, I’d like to quote a line from The Paper:

“Most of China’s yaoniang hide in the unnoticed cracks of society, far from everyone’s life yet very close to it at the same time.”

Here’s a little story. A girl was in an online relationship with someone, and things had progressed to the point where the two of them were really close. She felt it was time to come clean — if he could accept it, then it really was true love. So she said: I’m MtF.

The other person had no idea what that meant, and he was fine with it, so he looked it up online. And then he was dumbstruck. He said: I get it now, you’re a pervert — you don’t even count as a ladyboy, right?

She was baffled too. Then she looked at the screenshot he sent and understood. By definition, MtF is someone with a male body and a female heart — a male body, but a woman’s thoughts and inner self.

So what does the average person picture when they read that explanation?

A burly muscle-bound guy, or a wheezing otaku with stubble, smoking a cigarette while picking at his feet, then typing out a line that says: even though I look like this, inside I’m a girl.

That’s certainly what most people would picture. That isn’t the problem. The problem is that whether or not such a person actually exists, this image gets associated with the term.

That’s probably why MtF people can’t fit into this society. Society’s view of them is wrong, and some people look at this group through tinted glasses or with extreme, biased opinions.

Let me also talk about xyn (xiao yaoniang). The yaoniang community is actually very similar to MtF: they use medication, HRT (gender-affirming hormone therapy: taking the corresponding hormones to change their own sex-hormone status), or SRS (sex reassignment surgery: physically altering the appearance of their sex organs). After HRT/SRS treatment, most of them shift their appearance to match their psychological gender, put on the matching clothes, and are basically impossible to spot as transgender.

Here, as the OP Qijie, I should explain the difference between yaoniang and weiniang. The two differ by only one character, but their meanings are worlds apart. Let me start with weiniang: I sometimes am one, so I can define it clearly — a weiniang is someone who dresses up to look like a girl, which you could also call a “Cross dresser”. Plainly put, it’s dressing in drag; you see them most often at anime conventions, cosplaying as their beloved characters. And what is a yaoniang? I explained the definition above. Now look at the difference between the two — they’re completely different, aren’t they?

But Wong Kar-wai said: there is a kind of bird in this world that is born without legs, and it can only fly in the sky its whole life. When it gets tired, it sleeps in the wind. Such a bird can land only once in its entire life — when it dies.

Yaoniang are exactly that bird, unable to turn back and unable to land.

First of all, when a yaoniang’s sex characteristics begin to change, she has to conceal her identity. Once discovered, she faces one-sided attacks and rejection from the outside world — and family is no exception. Yes, even family is no exception. I’ve seen it myself.

According to the UNDP’s 2016 report Being LGBTI in China, sexual minorities in China have the highest incidence of discrimination within the family. That’s exactly why this group is so unwelcome in China.

What do yaoniang need? Hormones, of course. The hard life of adult yaoniang is already plain to see; the life of underage yaoniang is even more wretched.

Have you ever wondered where underage yaoniang who hide their identity earn the money to buy hormones?

This is a price list from Twitter; in reality it’s even more expensive.

At any time, capitalists reek from head to toe.

To outsiders, the fact that these yaoniang are underage makes it hard for capital to squeeze all their value out, so only shady brokers willing to take risks dare to fish in muddy waters. They think the most valuable thing about a yaoniang is her identity.

As underage yaoniang, they intersect with the ACG scene in the cosplay field. Some yaoniang peddle their own cosplay photos, and the bolder ones post photos of a more revealing kind. But selling photos is slow money in the end. Is there another way? Of course there is — money plus sex, you all get it (given the blog’s ICP filing, I won’t go into detail here, so that I don’t disappear). In this gray industry chain, the ones who make the most money usually aren’t them — it’s the brokers.

Why are young boys more often associated with the stereotype of being a girl? One good reason is that he wants to be, because he thinks the female stereotype is superior. For example, in a feminist family, masculinity may be ridiculed, or seen as a source of shame when the father is present. Even without such associations, children usually spend more time with their mothers. Although this connection only appears after most gender concepts have formed, schools mainly present children with female role models. Not to mention that many typical boy behaviors are often blamed in the modern school environment, and some are even suppressed with medication. So where does that leave boys today? Today, women can be anything they want. Women can be sporty girls, happy housewives, corporate executives and powerful politicians. There are two fashion categories: one is fashion for everyone, the other is fashion only for women. Women are seen as symbols of beauty and charm. Women are considered kind, gentle and empathetic, and are allowed to express their emotions freely. As traditional male advantages come to be seen as irrelevant or even negative, why wouldn’t boys rather be girls?

Of course, this is one reasonable explanation I can offer.

The gender identity issues and gender dysphoria that MtF people experience need attention and support from society and the medical community, including appropriate medical and psychological help. At the same time, we should recognize that gender identity and gender expression are diverse, and that everyone has the right to choose their own gender identity and way of expressing it. We should encourage inclusion and respect, and avoid discriminating against and excluding MtF people — because what kind of person you want to be is decided by yourself. A famous figure from the Renaissance said: go your own way and let others talk.

I believe that since it’s a path they chose themselves, they deserve respect — not the incomprehension and disgust of society.

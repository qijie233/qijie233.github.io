Why Involution in Society Keeps Getting Worse

Qijie
2023-04-20
Qijie's Ramblings
Economy, Society

To answer this question, you first have to know the cause of involution. Because the cause hasn’t changed, and it keeps pushing involution along, in that situation involution is only going to get worse and worse.

The cause of involution is that things aren’t organized well and aren’t distributed well. Bad forms of organization, bad ways of distributing, are driving involution to get worse and worse.

The cause of involution is not that there are too many people. Having many people is the foundation of society; why we need so many people has already been explained by the great leaders before us — go search for the answer yourself.

Why do students fail to get into middle school, high school, university? Some people put this down to too many people and involution. This question can actually be split apart into several questions:

  • You can’t learn it no matter how hard you try, so there’s no point studying — it only wastes time; otherwise you wouldn’t even be able to pass an exam.
  • You can learn it, but not as fast as others; school resources are limited, so only the people who learn best can be let in.
  • You learn more slowly than others. The state thinks you aren’t suited, and wants you to go into simple vocational education.

Students don’t hate the first case, but they do hate the second and third. Indeed, anything that goes against students’ wishes very likely won’t end well. Students don’t want to go into vocational and technical education; force them into it and they won’t learn anything there either — it only makes students feel they’ve been enslaved. And you’ve seen the result: plenty of students get sent off to factories to work as slaves.

As for case 2: if a student wants to learn and can learn, just more slowly than others, then in that case schools should add educational resources so these students can learn the knowledge they want. No educational resources? That’s impossible — the thing we have the most of right now is educational resources.

As for case 3: the state wants to arrange for some people to go into simple vocational education, and this is a big matter. First you have to change the mindset: everyone knows that getting into university is the mindset a person with ambition should have — not going to university means no ambition, and going into vocational education means no ambition. Second, after graduating from vocational education, most of those jobs don’t pay much.

People can’t find work, and some say it’s too many people and involution. That’s not it — it’s that there are many other people with your same professional skills. For the positions society has to offer, there are already several times too many of these people, and these people also haven’t been arranged into research or expanding the profession, haven’t been arranged into developing new technology. Many classmates are simply taught, and in the end “the crane and the clam fight, and the fisherman reaps the profit”. Failing the civil service exam is the same story. If everyone learned different professional skills, this wouldn’t happen.

Programmers working 996, delivery riders, bad business — it’s all because things aren’t organized well. Society hasn’t made good use of the advantage of having many people; instead it has turned having many people into a tool for benefitting itself at others’ expense. People really should think hard about why so many people are needed.

Some say involution is low-level competition. Its essence is that things aren’t organized well, so that competition happens at a low level instead of a high level. “Parents force their kids to memorize pi, and whoever memorizes more gets praised” — that’s low-level competition. In research institutions, when leaders raise the pay of people who give them gifts and don’t raise the pay of people who produce results, that’s encouraging low-level competition and opposing high-level competition.

You all know the theater effect: the people in front stand up, the people behind are forced to stand up too, and in the end everyone has spent the money, nobody watched the movie properly, and everyone is dead tired. If only someone would organize it. If only someone would make the people in front sit back down.

Some say “the essential causes of involution are: one, a relative shortage of high-quality resources; two, severely homogenized value orientation.” I agree with the second point — it’s because many people accept that “leaders raise the pay of people who give them gifts, and don’t raise the pay of people who produce results”. I don’t agree with the first point: money and power are called high-quality resources, but their essence is monopoly. So the relative shortage of high-quality resources is a surface phenomenon. The cause of involution is that things aren’t organized well and aren’t distributed well, and on top of that, monopoly appeared.

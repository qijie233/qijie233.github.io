Hands-On with a Lenovo Tiny PC (M6600q-N100)

Qijie
2024-11-02
Life & Misc › Tech Notes
Networking, PC, Review, Server, Ubuntu

0x0001

I'd been wanting to set up a NAS to play with for a while, and had been looking for a suitable tiny PC. Everything I saw on Xianyu was industrial PCs like the j4125, and I always felt their performance and looks didn't really match my needs — after all, I'm someone who has genuinely lived with a 2-core Pentium machine. In the end my sights settled on the tiny-PC category

My goal was for it to serve as a NAS and a small home server

I originally planned to buy HP's 4th-gen tiny PC, but I was afraid the performance wouldn't be enough; I wanted a 6th-gen, but it was a bit more expensive, so I kept wavering and hesitating the whole time, and in the end I gritted my teeth and chose this tiny PC I have now

My final choice of this tiny PC was obviously a decision made after weighing performance, price, appearance, and many other factors. Maybe the HP 4th-gen option looked quite tempting at first, but when you consider the balance of performance and price, you start looking for an option with better value for money. When you saw this tiny PC's performance and design, you probably felt it suited your needs better. You may also have had expectations for its expandability and upgradeability down the road, and finally decided to grit your teeth and choose this tiny PC.

0x0002

The specs are an i5-6600T low-voltage 4-core processor — after all, the whole machine only peaks at 65W, one single Hynix 4G 2166 memory stick, a Samsung 256G M.2 SATA drive, 450 RMB in total

To make it a proper NAS, I bought another Hynix memory stick with the same specs (-28 RMB) and a Seagate 2.5-inch 1T drive (-158 RMB), bringing the total price to 630 RMB (my wallet hurts so much!)

I'm very happy with the ports

Four USB 3.0 on the back, two USB 3.0 in front, a single Ethernet port, two DP. The only flaw is that there's no VGA or HDMI port — but then again, the seller threw one in, hehe

The internal structure is clear at a glance, and it's easy to take apart. The seller even included a 2.5-inch drive bracket. Inside there's one M.2 SATA port, one network card port (bottom right), and one 2.5-inch bay; there are two DDR4 memory slots, and I put in two Hynix 2166 sticks — anyway, running a NAS doesn't have much demand

What surprised me is that the 128G drive (M.2) mentioned on the seller's page turned out to be a 256G one when it reached my hands — and a Samsung at that! I looked into it specifically afterwards and the power-on time was only 8,100 hours, health 94%, but it doesn't show total writes and reads — strange

Peak power draw is 65W, and it definitely can't run at full load, so even at night the fan noise isn't very loud. Standby power draw is probably just a dozen-odd watts. What's that? You say the mechanical drive is loud? Just put the drive to sleep and that's it

It's a tiny PC after all — once I'd installed the system I just left it sitting there, and when necessary I use the TV as a monitor for emergencies (the cat is cute!)

Let me sum it up

Pros

  • Great looks — I fell in love with this tiny PC at first glance; the appearance alone adds a lot of points.

  • Great value for money — a desktop CPU converted from the laptop 6700H, performance and everything else is pretty good

  • Dual DDR4 memory slots; Lenovo brand machines feel premium and solid

  • You can replace the CPU yourself

Cons

  • So expensive

  • So expensive

  • So expensive

  • And after you take it home and want to expand it, you find out: so expensive

  • Relatively poor value for money

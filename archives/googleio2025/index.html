Google I/O Connect China

It's a real honor to be able to attend the Google Developer Conference

Keynote & Intelligent Leap Forward

The theme of this conference is Intelligent Leap Forward

Chen Junting, President of Google Greater China and Korea, was the first to take the stage and kicked off the conference. He said that Chinese developers are a dazzling force for innovation on the global stage, and that Google will keep helping Chinese developers achieve technical innovation and succeed in global markets.

Empowering developers with technology to accelerate innovation

For a long time, Google has been committed to providing technical and tooling support that injects momentum for innovation into developers, helping many developers going global from China keep pushing past boundaries and build quality products that benefit users worldwide. On this point, Chen Junting, President of Google Greater China and Korea, noted in his speech that Chinese developers going global have become an indispensable backbone of the global innovation stage. Whether startups or large enterprises, with the support of Google's full-stack AI solutions and global ecosystem, they have achieved remarkable success in global markets. The annual Google Play Best of lists show that 13 apps and games from 12 Chinese developer teams won 14 "Best App of the Year" awards across different markets worldwide, fully demonstrating the outstanding performance of Chinese developers going global in the global market.

AI: Build faster, unlock greater potential

From Gemini to Gemma, Google AI is innovating at an unprecedented pace. Timothy Jordan, Director of Developer Relations and Open Source at Google, introduced Google's most cutting-edge and most powerful AI models.

Gemini 2.5

Timothy Jordan gave developers a focused look at the thinking Gemini 2.5 family of models.

  • Gemini 2.5 Pro

Sets a new benchmark for coding ability and complex prompts

  • Gemini 2.5 Flash

Achieves the best balance of performance and speed, ideal for everyday tasks

  • Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite

A great fit for large-scale, low-cost workloads

Gemma: A powerful multilingual open model

Watching the Gemma demo on site, I was completely stunned by what it could do — this little 12b model is just that strong; it honestly doesn't feel like something a 12b should be able to pull off.

Downloading the model and trying it myself

After getting home, I deployed Gemma 3n E4b on LM Studio on my own PC. It's actually a 6.9B model (don't ask me why I didn't use the 12b — the answer is not enough VRAM. I didn't go for something like ollama that can run with GPU&CPU combined; to run it you first have to load it into VRAM, but the mobile 5060 only has 8G of VRAM). The model size is only 4.9G

The main things I tried it on were pulling solutions out of the document center for me, and role-play conversations with a Luo Tianyi preset (don't laugh)

  • It really is smart enough — it understands my questions and can find the correct solution in the documents; as long as you're not deliberately trying to stump it, it gives an accurate solution. As a privately deployed AI for everyday use, it's really quite good

  • For AI chat, maybe the lower bound of my preset settings is quite low (maybe). With the same preset, I feel it's even a bit cuter and smarter than Deepseek-v3

Overall, I'd say my rating of the Gemma models is pretty high

Gemma is the open model family Google launched, with core technology in the same lineage as the Gemini series. With contributions from developers worldwide, the Gemma family has been downloaded more than 200 million times to date, with over 80,000 derivative models contributed by the community

Chinese developers have used Gemma's multimodal and long-context capabilities to build all kinds of powerful results. To further empower innovation, Google launched a series of Gemma derivative models (Gemma Variants) for different application scenarios.

Impressive on-device performance is another big reason Gemma drew the attention of developers worldwide, and Google went further with Gemma 3n: built for excellent performance on mobile hardware, it runs smoothly on devices with as little as 2 GB of RAM. Although Gemma3n is lightweight, it shares the same architecture as Gemini Nano and its performance is no slouch either — it's an important step in Google's push for "AI for everyone".

AI optimizes the development toolchain across the board

Not only does Google put powerful AI models straight into developers' hands, it also weaves AI capabilities into every part of development, letting AI tools run through the entire tech stack and making development faster overall.

Flutter and Dart dual version updates:

Flutter 3.35 and Dart 3.9 are shipping this week, bringing "hot reload" to the web and turning it on by default. They also bring a brand-new "widget preview" and introduce the Dart MCP server, letting Gemini securely access the tools and context of your local development environment — transforming it from a code completion assistant into a true development partner that can analyze project errors, manage dependencies, and automatically update code.

Web: Native upgrades, AI takes the stage

Unleash the potential of native development! Paul Kinlan, Head of Chrome Developer Relations, introduced three directions of innovation for the Web platform: simplifying processes to build higher-quality UI; streamlining debugging with Chrome DevTools; and building brand-new features powered by AI.

Simplifying UI building, unleashing the Web's native potential

Carousels say goodbye to piles of JavaScript, and Baseline now covers the Web platform in full.

  • Brand-new CSS Primitives are introduced: with pseudo-classes like :scroll-button(*):scrollable-marker, developing interactive components such as carousels is greatly simplified, reducing the dependency on JavaScript.

  • Baseline is now integrated into mainstream development tools like VS Code, WebStorm and ESLint, and covers all Web platform features in full, achieving: checking feature availability in real time, so you don't have to switch pages back and forth when checking compatibility; and better support for non-Chrome users on Safari, Edge, Firefox and the like, helping the Web truly "develop once, run everywhere".

AI-powered debugging, efficiency multiplied

An AI assistant lands in Chrome DevTools, and the brutally hard Web development problem of CSS centering is now easily solved.

But the "hard" part isn't really the syntax — it's that there are so many scenarios and the details are so fragmented. The syntax is simple, the situations are complex; one line of code can solve it, but you have to pick the right line first.

Chrome DevTools AI assistant: with built-in Gemini, just describe your development goal in natural language and it can troubleshoot problems and generate suggested code fixes.

Baseline now covers all Web platform features in full: write code once for Chrome, Safari, Edge, Firefox and all kinds of browsers, and reach every user.

Connecting the community, growing together

Timothy Jordan, Director of Developer Relations and Open Source at Google, brought an important piece of news — the Google Developer Program is launching in China, offering local developers free learning resources, technical support, and community connections, helping everyone learn, build, and grow together in the global developer ecosystem.

An active developer community driving local innovation

China has a huge and vibrant developer community. From Beijing and Shanghai to Shenzhen, Google Developer Groups (GDG) around the country have built local tech exchange networks and hosted all kinds of great events, including "Build with AI" workshops, Study Jams, hackathons, and large-scale DevFest gatherings.

At the same time, the Google Developer Experts (GDE) community keeps growing — these experts, who have deep experience in various technical fields, keep helping the whole development ecosystem move forward through talks, open-source projects, and content creation

Qijie

At the exhibition area of the Google Developer Conference I chatted with the engineers on the Passkey team. They were amazed at how fast passkeys are recognized on my site — basically you tap to log in with a passkey, the dialog pops up, you pick Google Password Manager, verify your fingerprint, and you're logged in; the whole thing is smooth and takes under two seconds. That engineer from abroad asked what phone I use — a OnePlus — and you could tell he was genuinely surprised by that speed

But I tried it later: to use Google's password manager you have to turn on a VPN, so using it in China is still a bit difficult. I asked another guest whether Google will come back to China in the future; they said it's hard to say, and it's hard to have confidence about it

To be honest, my English listening is still pretty bad at the moment. I can express myself, but understanding and following along is still a bit of a struggle — it's not barrier-free communication, I'd call it half-barrier at best. The foreign engineer did understand what I meant, though. While we were chatting about tech face to face I didn't feel much, but thinking back on it later, thinking back on it later, it was pretty awkward.

Mood

I really envy people who get cared about. I've been short on love since I was little, yet I somehow trained myself in the skill of caring about other people — you could even say I care too much. Put in low-EQ terms: I'm a people-pleaser. Living like that is exhausting, because every day I have to act according to other people's faces; the moment someone treats me differently I can sense whether I'm disliked or seen as a nuisance, and then I start draining myself over what I did wrong

I was the host at my school for three years in high school, and people like that are theoretically incapable of being depressed — poised and elegant on any big occasion. The fact is I am: I started cutting myself back in middle school. Here's the cold joke — it wasn't until college that I went to the hospital on my own and came home with a moderate depression diagnosis, and I actually felt a little relieved: so these behaviors of mine come from an illness, and it's not that I'm “crazy”.

If — but there is no if — if my childhood could have changed the way my parents treated me, I wouldn't be like this. This isn't blame-shifting, because I know that since I was small my parents took outsiders' side, that they'd get physical at the slightest provocation when I was little, and in middle school they even threw a rock at me, leaving a scar on my right ulna where it got three stitches. I will remember that for the rest of my life. At the hospital I asked the doctor outright whether estradiol could be taken together with the medication I'm currently on; the doctor's reply was, I don't know, while endocrinology said it's fine. Then my father wanted to have me forcibly sent to a psychiatric hospital over the fact that I'm on E; when I feel awful I take anti-anxiety meds, and both my parents insist I'm taking pills at random. Showing them my prescription doesn't help either — they only accept medication prescribed by some inpatient doctor at Nanjing Brain Hospital, because she was the one in charge of me while I was hospitalized. They say it's for my own good, for my health. The problem is: where was that "for my own good" when I was in acute anxiety and feeling terrible

The subtext behind “I'm doing this for your own good” is “you must do as I say” — it's coercive, it stands for a parent's desire to control: my judgment is more accurate than yours, so I'll just make the decision for you, and you bear the bad consequences yourself, because their starting point is always good. If I really believed they were acting for my own good, why wouldn't I have obeyed obediently long ago? Good intentions don't necessarily make good outcomes, and the gap in understanding can't be closed. Here's a fairly counterintuitive one: parents generally won't say “I bought you that Cyberpunk (game), I'm doing this for your own good”; most of them will say “I've put your computer away, it interferes with your studies”. Different standpoints naturally produce different words. The generational gap can't be avoided; if it could be avoided, kids would surely be happier — they could tell their parents anything, and there'd be no depression at all. At home I don't dare say anything; as a kid I was afraid that one wrong sentence would get me scolded...

Right now I just want to finish college as soon as possible and then get far away from this family of origin,

Later on, I took all kinds of medication and nothing improved, but I was suffering so much — the anxiety and depression just wouldn't go away. I went bad; I learned to abuse medication. The brief euphoria it brings lets me escape the anxious, depressed state for a while, and what follows is an even bigger emptiness. Here Qijie advises everyone not to abuse medication — just advice.

I don't remember how many times I've died — maybe four (as of 26.8.17). Every time I swallowed pills, and every time I was saved. Modern medicine is just too advanced. The last time I lay in the ICU for four days, and came out again

About my gender

I'm MTF (Male To Female). As of today it's been almost HRT180 days. Unfortunately it got interrupted twice by hospital stays, for 12 days and 20 days. Being off E is painful; a closed psychiatric ward is even more painful

I hope the pronoun you use for me is She/Her. On a very important occasion it's fine to say “Mr. Qijie”. They all call me Sister Qijie

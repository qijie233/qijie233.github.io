Amateur Radio

The Exam

I sat the Class A amateur radio exam in Xuzhou (Jiangsu) on April 29. I started reviewing (or should I say previewing & studying) the exam questions three days in advance

If you want a crash-course method, take a look at this article➡️ (though I didn't really read it)

My strategy was to go through the question bank once and then jump straight into mock exams — thirty mocks a day (or even more). At first, Qijie heard from a friend that finishing thirty questions in under three minutes was unbelievable, but later on, Qijie's fastest and best result was one minute fifty seconds, 28 of 30 correct. Unreal. That only happened because I got a set of questions I knew inside out. There were still ones I didn't know after that, but once you're fluent you can generally finish in three and a half to four minutes

Xuzhou uses computer-based testing with the score given on the spot. I finished in four minutes, got 28 right and passed. The electronic operator certificate was issued on the evening of April 30, but for the physical operator certificate, for some reason the Xuzhou staff told me it's currently suspended

Which is pretty strange?

Setting Up a Station

Although my area is connected to Nanjing's rail transit (the metro), on paper it's still under Zhenjiang's jurisdiction. Honestly, from here I can't hit the Zhenjiang repeater, but I can hit the Nanjing one. That's rather thought-provoking.

But since my place is still under Zhenjiang's jurisdiction, my amateur radio station license has to be handled in Zhenjiang

Here I have to criticize the Zhenjiang Industry and Information Bureau: it can't be done on the Jiangsu government services website — ask why, and the answer is that they haven't integrated that function, so it's in-person only. But the license is issued on the spot too, and in-person processing is actually pretty fast, about ten-plus minutes. The materials you need are

  • A photocopy of the Class A operator certificate (I just used a screenshot of the electronic certificate)

  • ID card

  • Handheld radio (station) type approval certificate (the CMITD must be legible)

  • Handheld radio (check the S/N code behind the battery)

The best part is!!! The call signs issued in Zhenjiang are five characters!!! Five characters!! That counts as rare, and the communication range is global (inter-provincial and foreign-related); in some places you can only get within-province. I feel so lucky to be in Zhenjiang 😭

But they told me that in the second half of this year (2025) the license is going to be changed (they didn't really explain it clearly)

Why I Recommend Reading Articles Before Watching Videos

Qijie
2023-10-29
Tech Notes
Reading

Introduction

In this fast-paced life the amount of information is ballooning like crazy, and the way we get information has gone from text-and-images to video, and then to short video.

This post analyzes the pros and cons of two media — text-and-images and video — to help you weigh them better and pick the right channel for getting information.

Without further ado, let’s start with video!

Video

Pros: the combination of picture and sound strengthens the impression, which is the best for remembering hands-on knowledge like running experiments.

Cons: when you’re watching study (serious) videos and you can’t keep up with the instructor, you want to go back or slow down, and it turns out the progress bar is a pain to control. You can change the playback speed, but rewinding really is annoying (tapping the <- key a few times). And when you understand faster than the instructor speaks, once you speed up you can’t hear clearly what they’re saying. Really annoying.

What’s more tiresome here is short video — the recommendation style is force-feeding, and on top of that it decides which videos you like based on how long you linger on videos in each category. Bit by bit the short-video platform learns what you want and pushes exactly that (don’t get any ideas), so you keep watching and scrolling, and while you scroll, a little of everyone’s lifespan slips away (no joke).

Also, making a video takes far more time and energy than writing a text-and-image post. Making one starts with collecting material and ends with post-production editing, and that process can wear plenty of people out.

Text and Images

Pros: you can freely adjust your reading speed to match your comprehension, you can “save your game” and stop for a rest (pause a video and there’s a good chance you won’t know what it’s doing when you come back), and you can even skip the parts you already know, avoiding wasting time on what you know.

Cons: for people whose comprehension isn’t great, you usually still need video to deepen understanding. You might also get sleepy (most people do, so it’s probably normal). And in some situations you have to hear the sound to catch the timing, and then you must watch a video to get it.

Of course, if you can understand it through comics and the like, even better.

Also, commands and the like given in a video can’t be copied directly, so text-and-images win by a bit on this point.

What? Someone mentions OCR. But if what gets recognized is sudo rm -rf / var/log/* (a dangerous command that deletes / (the root directory) — please don’t try it in production), and you paste it in and run it without even looking, then in the end it’s basically a joke.

So: if you can read the text and images first, do that, and when you hit something you can’t understand, it’s not too late to watch a video.

The Author’s Rambling

When the author learns something new, they always read the relevant articles and books first. When the author themselves finds it hard to grasp, they hit a search engine and watch videos to understand the material better (usually things about hands-on operations).

Final Words

This post is for reference only, and results vary from person to person — but it’s worth a try.

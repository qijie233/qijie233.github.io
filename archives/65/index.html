Rainy Days — Do You Like Them?

Qijie
2023-04-18
Reflections
Daily Life

I quite like rainy days.

The pattering drizzle moistens all things in silence; the lightning-quick downpour scours the world clean.

Rain in a hundred guises falls in a hundred postures; people in a thousand moods walk through ten thousand rains.

How should a life be spent? Where should I go? What should I do? Will my wishes come true? Where… does my path lie?

Rainy days always calm me down.

Even though a cold front usually brings the chill of a high-pressure air mass afterwards, and the damp, muddy paths after the rain always leave me wondering how on earth to skirt the puddles perfectly…… those little bothers still can’t outweigh what I hear and feel on a rainy day.

Growing up on the coastal plain of the lower Yangtze, the rain in my memory is mostly gentle. Apart from the downpours nature gifts us during peak typhoon season in summer and autumn, most of the time the rain comes down quietly and evenly, as if it leaves the dark clouds and drops straight onto your head.

But the spring rain in old poems always feels like it slants into the world, always managing to fly onto the desk of a poet by the window, dampening a few sheets of paper and stirring up longing. Take “it steals in with the wind at night, moistening things without a sound” — “steals” is a verb here; it feels like the rain is stooping low to slip into the courtyard, never standing straight, never upright.

Right — it is spring rain after all, and spring wind always comes with spring rain. Maybe it’s precisely the wind’s guidance that lets spring rain moisten all things without a sound. (If it fell straight and flat, it probably couldn’t take care of the roots under the tender shoots.)

Another example: “black clouds spill like ink, not yet hiding the hills; white rain leaps like pearls, scattering into the boat.” The raindrops bounce, like a handful of glass beads tossed down, springing apart again as they hit the ground and splashing everywhere, full of momentum… that’s not falling straight either.

Speaking of raindrops, I suddenly think of “Song of the Pipa”. In a mood like this, reading it again — “the thick strings clatter like a sudden rain, the thin strings whisper like private talk; clattering and whispering, they play all jumbled together, big pearls and small pearls falling onto a jade plate” — I find a different feeling again. Maybe with a little editing, swapping the thing described and the thing it’s described with, it would turn into a strange poem about rain… but that would more or less waste the talent of the Sima of Jiangzhou. A “Rain of the Pipa” would at most let me get a bit sentimental through the rain; but the “Song of the Pipa” cries out the poet’s pain and oppression that had been pent up inside him for so long. He was so desolate that the echo has circled the beams and never died, for a thousand years……

Following the whim, I looked up where “white rain leaps like pearls, scattering into the boat” comes from. Turns out it’s Su Dongpo’s poem: “Written While Drunk at Wanghu Tower on the 27th Day of the Sixth Month”. At that time, Mr. Dongpo had placed second on the imperial exam list, had just entered official life, and in the fourth year of Xining was sent to Hangzhou to take up a post. Hangzhou is a place of rich water and soil; being able to go there on your first official posting was a sign of the emperor’s regard and a smooth career… Probably, that day’s “written while drunk” was in exactly such high spirits…… If the Dongpo who had just come out of the Wutai Poetry Case had written it, would the rainy day he wrote down then still have had this much motion……

I’m sleepy, time to rest. I’ll leave this little essay here.

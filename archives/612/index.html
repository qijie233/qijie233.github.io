SSTV: A Way to Transmit Images with Audio, and Its Special Uses

Qijie
2024-04-18
Tech Notes
SSTV

Introduction

SSTV, short for Slow-Scan TeleVision, is commonly seen in the amateur radio world.
It’s a method of transmitting images that began in the mid-to-late 20th century; nowadays you can encode and decode SSTV audio signals with a PC and the right software.

Common modes include Martin 1, the Robot series, the PD series and so on; different modes support different resolutions and colors.

More detailed information about SSTV can be obtained from a search engine.

Characteristics & Special Uses

Its drawbacks are that it’s blurry (low resolution) and slow, but you can encode a QR Code into SSTV to get around the blurriness.

Once a QR Code is encoded as an SSTV audio signal, the QR code can be sent in audio form,

which makes it possible to send a “QR code” as audio in some QQ groups where the group bot doesn’t allow links or images containing QR codes.

Of course, SSTV is sometimes used as a way for games to hide easter eggs. For example, in the game Portal there’s an easter egg that appears in SSTV form (in the first game it’s a radio).

Decode

Use the Receive page; one click lets you load an audio file or listen to the sound playing on the computer to read SSTV audio.

When encoding, the speakers make a noise like an old modem; you can turn the volume down separately in the volume control (this doesn’t affect the result when outputting to a file).

In most cases the image doesn’t need to be large; resolutions below 1024x1024 work better. (Larger images get their resolution scaled down automatically to fit.)

I generally recommend using the “Fit” image filling method with Mode “PD120/180” or “Martin 1” for encoding.
